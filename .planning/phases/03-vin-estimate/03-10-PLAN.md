<design_analysis>
## The mechanism: what `absolute inset-0` actually does inside a scroller

`inset-0` resolves against the **padding box of the nearest positioned ancestor**. For the
estimate `<section>` that ancestor is the section itself (`relative`). The section is `h-dvh`,
so its padding box is a fixed 100dvh-tall box — the padding box does **not** grow to the
height of overflowing scroll content. Consequence:

If `overflow-y-auto` is put on the `<section>` while `<Image fill>` and
`<div className="absolute inset-0 bg-black/55" />` stay as direct children:
- both layers remain exactly 100dvh tall, covering only the first viewport's worth of the
  scrollable area, and
- being `absolute` (not `fixed`/`sticky`) they are positioned in the scroll content flow, so
  they **scroll away** with it.

Scrolling down inside the section would slide the photo and the dark overlay upward and expose
bare `<body>` background beneath the card. That is the seam the gap brief warned about, and it
is why the `ServicesSection` precedent transfers only partially: `ServicesSection` has a flat
`bg-background` on the section itself (a background *paint*, which repeats over the scrollable
area) and no absolutely-positioned layers at all.

## The four candidates

| # | Approach | Verdict | Reason |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| (a) | `overflow-y-auto` on `<section>` + make the backdrop cover the full scroll area | **Ruled out** | Requires wrapping both backdrop layers in a new element sized to the scroll content. There is no CSS length for "scroll content height", so it means `h-full` on a wrapper that only works if the wrapper is itself the flow content — i.e. restructuring the layer stack and re-deriving the z-index order. Highest blast radius for zero user-visible gain over (d). |
| (b) | `overflow-y-auto` on `<section>` + `sticky top-0 h-dvh` backdrop wrapper | **Ruled out** | Works in principle, but introduces a sticky layer that must be a flow sibling of the card (sticky does not apply to `position: absolute` children), so the card can no longer be centred by the section's own flexbox. Changes desktop layout mechanics to fix a mobile-only defect — violates hard constraint 3. |
| (c) | Keep `overflow-hidden`, `items-center` → `items-start` + vertical padding | **Ruled out** | Cheapest, and it does stop the *top* from being clipped. But it changes desktop appearance (the card would sit at the top-padding offset instead of optically centred, on every viewport) and the **bottom** stays clipped with no scroller — the reset button and disclaimer become unreachable at 667px. Trades one clipped end for the other. |
| (d) | Keep the `<section>` `relative overflow-hidden h-dvh`; put the scroller on an inner wrapper around the card | **CHOSEN** | The section keeps its exactly-viewport-sized padding box, so `inset-0` on both backdrop layers resolves identically to today — the photo and overlay are provably untouched, no wrapper, no sticky, no z-index change. Only the card's own wrapper gains a height cap plus scroll. |

## Why (d) does not reintroduce the nested-scroll hazard as an always-on scroller

The scroller uses **`max-h-dvh overflow-y-auto`**, not `h-full overflow-y-auto`. `max-h`
leaves the element at its natural content height whenever that height is under the cap, so:
- **Desktop / >= ~824px tall:** the card is shorter than `max-h-dvh`. The wrapper never
  reaches its cap, produces no scrollable overflow, and `overflow-y-auto` renders no
  scrollbar and captures no wheel/touch events. The section's `flex items-center justify-center`
  still centres the wrapper exactly as today. **Pixel-identical to current output** — this is
  the concrete guarantee for hard constraint 3, and it is a property of `max-h` rather than a
  media query, so it degrades on real content height instead of a guessed breakpoint.
- **375x667 with the tall result card:** the card exceeds the cap, the wrapper clamps to the
  viewport height and becomes scrollable. Content starts at the wrapper's top edge, so the
  section heading and the vehicle headline are the first things rendered and are always
  visible; the disclaimer and reset button are reachable by scrolling down inside the card.
  Nothing is clipped at either end.

This is strictly narrower than `ServicesSection`'s always-on inner `overflow-y-auto`, so it
does not make the pre-existing nested-scroll perception worse (hard constraint 4).

## The `motion.div` and the scrollRef IO root

The `motion.div` that owns `viewport={{ root: scrollRef, ... }}` must **not** become the
scroller. Motion's `whileInView` uses an IntersectionObserver with `root: scrollRef`; the
observed element must be a descendant of that root, and introducing a new scroll parent
*between* the root and the target changes the intersection geometry that the observer reports.
Keeping the scroll on a **new wrapper placed outside the `motion.div`** — between the
`<section>` and the `motion.div` — leaves the `motion.div` in exactly the same position in
the containing chain relative to `scrollRef` for the section-entrance case, because at
entrance time the section is scrolled to the top of the wrapper (scrollTop 0) and the
`motion.div` occupies the wrapper's full width at its natural offset.

Executor: place the scroll wrapper **outside** the `motion.div`, and verify the animation
still fires (acceptance criterion below). If the animation regresses, that is a real signal —
report it rather than removing `viewport={{ root: scrollRef }}`.
