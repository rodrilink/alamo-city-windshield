scroller on that page, while leaving `/about` and `/contact` as ordinary scrolling

## Locked decision being implemented

"Home owns the viewport; nav overlays." The snap container in `page.tsx` becomes the only
scroller on `/`. `TopNav` is drawn on top of the sections instead of consuming vertical
space. The Footer moves out of the layout's normal flow and becomes the last snap stop
*inside* the container.

## Chosen mechanism: move chrome composition down into the pages

`(public)/layout.tsx` becomes a passthrough (renders `{children}` only). Each of the three
public pages composes the chrome it needs:

- `/` renders `<TopNav />` as an absolutely-positioned overlay plus the snap container,
  whose last child is a Footer snap stop.
- `/about` and `/contact` each render the exact `flex min-h-screen flex-col` +
  `TopNav` + `main.flex-1` + `Footer` structure that the shared layout provides today.

**Why this over the alternatives:**

- **`usePathname()` conditional in the layout** — would force `(public)/layout.tsx` to
  become a Client Component. That pushes the whole public route group client-side and
  makes `/about` and `/contact` (currently server-rendered) lose static rendering. Too
  much blast radius for a CSS-height bug.
- **A new nested route group** (e.g. `(marketing)/`) — requires moving `about/` and
  `contact/` directories on disk. Larger diff, more chance of breaking imports, and the
  route group nesting buys nothing that per-page composition doesn't.
- **Per-page composition** — three small, explicit files. Each page states its own chrome,
  so there is no hidden coupling between `/` and the other two routes. The duplication is
  ~8 lines repeated twice, which is cheaper than the correctness risk of the other options.

## Footer treatment on home: fourth snap stop, content-sized, aligned to its end

**USER DECISION (2026-08-05) — locked.** Two options were rejected before this one; both are
recorded so the executor does not reintroduce either.

**Rejected: nesting the Footer inside `ServicesSection`.** That section is already
`h-dvh overflow-y-auto bg-background flex items-center` with a 4-card grid, 3 testimonials,
and a CTA. Appending the Footer pushes content past `h-dvh` and forces scrolling *within* a
snap section, making the wheel ambiguous between advancing the snap and scrolling inner
overflow. That fights `snap-mandatory`. This objection stands.

**Rejected: omitting the Footer from `/`.** The Footer carries `BUSINESS.hours`,
`BUSINESS.location`, `BUSINESS.serviceArea`, and `BUSINESS.phone`. For a local service
business landing page that is primary content and locally SEO-relevant, not chrome — a
homepage visitor looking for hours must not have to navigate to `/about`. Dropping it is a
product decision, and the user declined it.

**Chosen: the Footer becomes a fourth snap stop** inside the snap container, after
`ServicesSection`, sized to its own content.

### Sizing: not `h-dvh`

`src/components/layout/Footer.tsx` is a compact 3-column grid (brand/service-area, hours,
contact) plus a copyright bar at `py-8 md:py-12`. Measured against `BUSINESS` in
`src/lib/constants.ts` — 3 hours rows, a one-line service area, a one-line location, phone,
copyright — its natural height is roughly 260–320px on desktop and ~400–450px at 375px wide
once `md:grid-cols-3` collapses to `grid-cols-1`. Both fit inside a 667px viewport with room
to spare, so the stop needs no internal scrolling at the mobile target. Stretched to a full
viewport it would read as sparse and broken.

### The mechanic that matters: `snap-end`, not `snap-start`

This is the detail the revision brief flagged as genuinely open. Resolved here rather than
left to the executor:

With `snap-y snap-mandatory`, a stop shorter than the scrollport is hazardous **as the last
child**. Under `snap-start` the browser wants the Footer's *top* edge aligned to the
container's top edge — but the scroller cannot scroll past its content end. Maximum scroll
leaves the Footer's top edge partway down the viewport with the tail of `ServicesSection`
still visible above it, so `snap-mandatory` holds a target it can never satisfy. That is
exactly the "partial section visible above it" artifact to avoid, and it can make the last
wheel gesture feel sticky or bounce back.

**`snap-end` aligns the stop's bottom edge to the scrollport's bottom edge — precisely where
maximum scroll lands.** The target is always satisfiable, the snap resolves cleanly, and the
Footer rests flush against the bottom of the viewport with the bottom of `ServicesSection`
filling the space above it. Snapping back up to `ServicesSection` is unaffected because that
section keeps its own `snap-start` alignment.

The three existing sections keep `snap-start snap-always` untouched. `snap-always` goes on
the Footer wrapper too, so a fast fling cannot skip past it.

### Concrete treatment

The Footer stop is `<section className="snap-end snap-always shrink-0">` wrapping
`<Footer />`, and nothing else.

- **No `h-dvh` / `min-h-*`** — content-sized, per the brief.
- **No `overflow-y-auto`** — this is what guarantees no nested-scroll ambiguity. With no
  overflow container there is no inner scroller for the wheel to capture, so a wheel event
  over the Footer can only drive the snap container.
- **`shrink-0`** — the snap container is a block container, not flex, so this is
  belt-and-braces; it costs nothing and stops a future `flex` on the parent from compressing
  the stop.
- **`snap-end`** — per the analysis above.

`src/components/layout/Footer.tsx` is **not modified**, so `/about` and `/contact` keep
rendering it byte-identically. All snap behavior lives on the wrapper `<section>` in
`page.tsx`.

## Nav overlay mechanics

`TopNav`'s root `<nav>` currently carries `sticky top-0 z-50`. `sticky` is what makes it
consume a row in the flex column. To keep `/about` and `/contact` behavior identical while
letting `/` overlay it, `TopNav` takes an optional `overlay?: boolean` prop:

- `overlay` unset/false (the `/about`, `/contact` case) → `sticky top-0` exactly as today.
- `overlay` true (the `/` case) → `absolute top-0` so it is removed from flow and painted
  over the hero. `z-50` and the existing
  `bg-background/95 backdrop-blur supports-[backdrop-filter]:bg-background/60` classes stay
  in both modes, so it remains legible over the hero photo.

`absolute` (not `fixed`): the nav is positioned against the home page's `relative` wrapper,
which is exactly one viewport tall and is not itself scrolled. `fixed` would work visually
too, but `absolute` keeps the nav out of the scroll container entirely and avoids any
interaction with the snap container's scroll position on mobile browsers where `fixed`
elements shift with the visual viewport.

Click-through is bounded by the nav's own box: the root `<nav>` gets `w-full` and its inner
`div` is `h-16`. Nothing below the 64px band is covered, because the `<nav>` has no height
beyond its content and no `inset-0`. Do not add `h-dvh`, `inset-0`, or a full-height
wrapper around it.
